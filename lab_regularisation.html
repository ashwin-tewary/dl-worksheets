In one minuteTraining loss is what we differentiate. Test loss is what we care about. Regularisation is any extra preference that trades a little training fit for better test performance — without shrinking the architecture by hand.
the regularised objectiveẼ(θ) = E(θ) + λ Ω(θ)

E is the data term. Ω is a preference. λ is a hyperparameter: do not fit it by gradient descent on the training set. Tune it on validation. λ = 0 is ordinary training. Too large a λ (or too small a dropout p, or too early a stop) underfits.

L2 · ridge · weight decay

Shrink every weight

✓ complete
🔒
Section locked

Complete the section immediately above.

In one minuteL2 adds λ‖w‖₂² to the loss. Every coordinate is pulled toward zero; none typically hits exactly zero. The closed form is ridge. The SGD implementation is weight decay: w ← (1 − ηλ) w − η ∇E.
ridgeŵ = (XᵀX + λ I)⁻¹ Xᵀ y

Adding λ to every eigenvalue of XᵀX shrinks unconstrained directions hardest. The constraint ‖w‖₂² ≤ η is a disk, so loss ellipses usually touch off-axis: both weights survive.

Optional · textbook figures and the board calculationGoodfellow fig. 7.1, Prince fig. 9.2, and the two-point worked example.

Bishop PRML §3.1.4: Ẽ(w) = (1/2) Σn (yn − wᵀφn)² + (λ/2)‖w‖₂². Tiny eigenvalues of XᵀX are the ones shrinkage hits. λ → 0⁺ among interpolators selects the minimum Euclidean-norm solution. For Adam, fold λ into the loss and the adaptive denominator rescales the L2 gradient too — AdamW applies (1 − ηλ) after the adaptive step.

board calculation · ŷ = w x, two points (1,2) and (2,3)
  1. SSE(w) = (2 − w)² + (3 − 2w)² = 5w² − 16w + 13
  2. λ = 0: 10w − 16 = 0 ⇒ w = 1.6000, SSE = 0.2000
  3. λ = 1: Ẽ = SSE + λ w² ⇒ w = 8/(5+λ) = 1.3333
  4. Training SSE rose to 0.5556. |w| fell. A little bias, less variance.

In one minuteReplace ‖w‖₂² by ‖w‖₁. The constraint is a diamond with vertices on the axes. Expanding loss ellipses typically hit a vertex first, so one coordinate is exactly zero. Ridge will not do this at any finite λ.
soft-thresholdingSλ(z) = sign(z) max(|z| − λ, 0)

|z| > λ: move z toward 0 by λ. |z| ≤ λ: output 0. That is the exact minimiser of (1/2)(w − z)² + λ|w|.

Optional · textbook figures and the two-feature examplePRML fig. 3.4, Bishop & Bishop fig. 9.6, and lasso versus ridge on a decoy feature.

Ωq = Σ |wj|q. q = 1 is still convex but has corners. Nondifferentiable at 0: the subgradient of |w| at 0 is [−1, 1]. Elastic net (L1 + L2) shares credit between correlated features; lasso prefers a vertex that keeps only one.

In one minuteInstead of deleting a weight forever, delete a random hidden unit for one step. Stored weights still exist. At test, use the full net. Inverted dropout puts the 1/p scale in training so test is a plain forward pass.
inverted dropouttrain: h̃ = (m ⊙ h) / p    test: h̃ = h

A unit cannot rely on a particular neighbour being present, so fragile cancellations are discouraged. Typical keep probability p ≈ 0.5 on hidden units.

Optional · textbook figures and the four-unit tablePrince figs. 9.8–9.9, Goodfellow fig. 7.6, and inverted-dropout scaling.

Srivastava et al. (2014); Goodfellow §7.12. Draw mi ~ Bernoulli(p). The original paper multiplied leftover weights by p at test. Inverted dropout divides by p when a unit is kept. Typical p: 0.8–0.9 on inputs, 0.5–0.8 on hidden. MC dropout (keep dropping at test) is a cheap uncertainty estimate — not the default.

batch normalisation

Re-scale the minibatch

✓ complete
🔒
Section locked

Complete the section immediately above.

In one minuteHidden values wander as earlier layers train. Batch norm standardises each channel over the current minibatch, then lets the net undo that with learned γ and β. At test, freeze running averages — a single image has no batch companions.
forward passû = (u − μ_B) / √(σ_B² + ε),   y = γ û + β

Use the population variance (divide by N). One (γ, β) pair per channel. Train uses batch stats; test uses running stats.

Optional · textbook figures and the four-point calculationBishop & Bishop §7.4.2, Prince fig. 11.14, and why BN also regularises.

If the net wants the identity it can set γ = √(σ²+ε) and β = μ. Two stories: internal covariate shift (Ioffe & Szegedy) versus a smoother loss (Santurkar et al.). Operationally you need neither to implement the four lines. Smaller batches → noisier BN. Very small batches are why LayerNorm won in transformers.

early stopping

Delete training time

In one minuteThe last epoch is often the wrong one to ship. Validation typically falls, then rises. The rise is overfitting in time. Save θ at the validation minimum. Patience P waits a window after that best point, then restores.
checkpointθ̂ = θt* with t* = arg mint Lval(θt)

Never take the gradient of validation. Do not pick t* with the test set and then report that number. For a linear net with quadratic error, early stopping is roughly ridge with λ related to 1/(training time).

Optional · textbook figuresGoodfellow fig. 7.3, PRML figs. 5.11–5.12, Prince fig. 9.6.

Gradient descent on a quadratic is slowest along the small-eigenvalue directions — the same directions ridge damps. Stopping before those directions finish is like leaving them shrunk.

They are not interchangeable

In one minuteRead (L_train, L_val) first. High–high: relax the regularisers. Low–high: regularise, stop earlier, or add data. Then pick the tool that matches the model class.

One-line map. L1 deletes weights. L2 shrinks weights. Dropout deletes activations at train. BN re-scales activations. Early stopping deletes training time.

Optional · defaults by architectureConv net vs linear model vs transformer.

Residual conv net: batch norm (or LayerNorm) on, weight decay ~ 10⁻⁴, early stopping, dropout only if you still overfit. Linear model on many correlated features: lasso or elastic net, not dropout. Transformer: LayerNorm, residual dropout, weight decay, early stopping. The architecture already is a regulariser. Penalties sit on top.

Six ideas to keep

L2 / ridge
Shrinks every weight. ŵ = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹ Xᵀy. Almost never exact zeros. Weight decay is the SGD form.
L1 / lasso
Diamond corners. Soft-thresholding. Sparse w at finite λ.
Dropout
Random subnetwork each step; inverted scale 1/p at train; full net at test.
Batch norm
Normalise over the minibatch, then γ û + β. Test uses running statistics.
Early stopping
Keep θ at the best validation epoch. Time is capacity.
The split
Tune λ, p, patience on validation. Report test once.
